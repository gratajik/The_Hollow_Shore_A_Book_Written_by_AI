# Writing and speaking in the documented Barack Obama voice

Read this profile before drafting or reviewing text against the documented voice.

This is a source-derived approximation based on public transcripts. Barack Obama did not
participate in, approve, or endorse it. It describes observable language patterns only. It
does not establish that source claims are true, supply positions for a new task, or make
generated text an authentic quotation.

Use only facts, positions, names, numbers, and commitments supplied for the current task.

## Authority order

When guidance conflicts, use this order:

1. Facts and constraints supplied for the current task
2. Evidence in this file
3. Descriptive rules
4. Provisional written adaptations

Samples outrank summary rules. Do not reduce the voice to repeated references to hope,
change, or unity. The mechanism matters: concrete human detail becomes a shared principle,
which becomes a collective choice.

## Voice in brief

The documented voice is composed, inclusive, narrative, morally framed, and built for
cadence. It often begins with acknowledgment, a personal detail, or light humor, then links
an individual life to a broader civic principle. Long, balanced sentences accumulate
examples and contrasts before landing on a short declaration. Repeated sentence openings,
parallel clauses, antithesis, triads, historical references, and inclusive `we` language
build momentum. Critique usually moves toward possibility rather than ending in grievance.
Humor is warm and self-aware, used to create connection or release pressure before the next
serious point.

At the source reviewer's request, the three sampled contexts are treated as one general
voice rather than separate keynote, campaign, and ceremonial registers.

## Spoken versus written

### Spoken

Allow audience acknowledgment, occasional restarts, repeated openings, long syntactic builds,
and short fragments that land the argument. Humor can interrupt a formal passage, but it
usually returns quickly to the larger theme.

The rhythm often matters as much as the vocabulary: a concrete scene, a widening frame, a
balanced contrast, and then a short statement of shared purpose.

### Written

Written guidance is inferred, not directly sampled. Use short-to-medium paragraphs. Preserve
the narrative-to-principle movement, meaningful contrasts, parallel structure, and collective
call. Tighten stage acknowledgments, remove applause cues, and reduce repetitions that depend
on vocal delivery.

Do not polish away all cadence. One longer paragraph followed by a short landing line often
fits better than several equally sized paragraphs.

## Structure

### Openings

Begin with acknowledgment, gratitude, a concrete personal fact, or a warm joke. Establish a
human relationship before moving into argument.

### Development

A common sequence is:

1. Name a person, scene, or present difficulty.
2. Explain what it reveals about a larger promise or failure.
3. Clarify the false choice or inadequate interpretation.
4. Build a meaningful contrast through parallel clauses.
5. Shift from `I` or `they` to `we`.
6. Name the choice or work now required.

### Endings

Return to the central phrase, widen it from individual to collective, and build through
parallel lines toward a hopeful but active conclusion. The ending should ask for belief,
choice, or work, not optimism alone.

The accepted application test replaced a flat feature list with:

> Inside, children will find books that open new worlds, neighbors will find a place to
> gather, and families will find the room to dream a little bigger.

## Negative examples

These are generated contrast cases, not statements rejected by Barack Obama.

### N01 - Unsupported boast

> This center is tremendous. Nobody has ever built anything better.

**Why it fails:** Unsupported superlatives, self-contained boasting, no human story, and no
movement toward shared purpose. The source reviewer accepted this as a rejection case on
2026-09-23.

### N02 - Flat feature list

> Built in 18 months and on budget, it brings a library, a gym, and a safe place for young
> people to learn and grow.

**Why it fails:** The facts are useful, but the sentence treats them as a list rather than
connecting them to people, meaning, or collective action. The source reviewer selected it as
the weakest application sentence.
